ElectraLab | Earthing designer Pro

Facility templates with BNBC / IEC / IEEE basis, rod, pit and grid sizing, bonding schematic and detail drawings.

What should I enter?
  • Application: what is being earthed. It sets the target resistance, fault current and whether a pit is mandatory. Edit the target if your spec differs.
  • Soil resistivity: use a measured value from a 4-pole Wenner test. Without one, pick the nearest soil type as a first estimate.
  • Soil model: pick Two-layer if your Wenner readings change a lot with spacing. Enter the top layer value above, the bottom layer value and the top layer depth.
  • Dry-season factor: 1.0 if you measured in the dry season, 1.3 or 1.6 if you measured in the monsoon.
  • Target resistance: from the project spec or utility. Typical values: substation 1 Ω, LPS 10 Ω, building 5 Ω.
  • Fault current, share and time: the ground fault current at this installation, from the utility or a fault study, not the 3-phase level. Share into soil is the part returning through the earth rather than cable sheaths or earth wires. Leave 100% if unknown.
  • Electrode type: copper-bonded rod is the default. GI pipe is common locally.
  • Available rod lengths and maximum count: enter the lengths actually available from your supplier. The calculator evaluates combinations of those lengths and rod counts up to the limit you set. It does not optimize bore depth separately.
  • Pit spacing: leave blank for auto, about twice the rod length up to 6 m.
  • Backfill and surface layer: choose what you will actually install. Crushed rock raises the tolerable touch voltage.
  • Down conductors: enter the count for LPS. Each one gets its own pit.
  • Configuration: leave on Auto, or pick a layout.
  • Compare configurations: see which layout meets the target with the least metal, then press Use to switch to it.
Calculation method
  • Design soil: measured resistivity × dry-season factor.
  • Two-layer soil: rods use L / (h1/ρ1 + (L − h1)/ρ2). Strip, ring and grid use the same average over an effective depth of 3 to 15 m.
  • Rods: Dwight formula. Pits use a backfill column model.
  • Rod groups: mutual-resistance group factor for rods in a row.
  • Rod + strip: buried strip formula combined with the rods, 15% interaction penalty.
  • Ring earth: Sunde ring formula with 4 corner rods.
  • Earth grid: Sverak formula (IEEE 80). Mesh spacing and burial depth come from the library.
  • Conductor size: S = I√t / k (Cu 159, steel 58).
  • Touch and step: ground potential rise = share × fault current × resistance, compared with IEEE 80 limits for a 70 kg person. Screening only, no decrement factor.

How to read this table
  • What this shows: all five layouts run on the same soil, target resistance and fault inputs, so you can compare them before choosing.
  • ✓ and ✗: ✓ means the target is met. ✗ shows the best resistance reached within the evaluated limits. Rod layouts use the available lengths and maximum rod count entered in Design inputs; strip, ring and grid options remain approximate layout comparisons.
  • Metal (m): total length of rods, strip and conductor. It is a rough size indicator, not a cost. Deep rods cost more per metre than trench strip, and rings and grids need open ground.
  • Use: switches the design, results and drawings to that layout. Auto picks the best rod layout and only falls back to a grid when rods cannot reach the target.
  • Site fit: the comparison does not know your available area, obstructions or local practice. Check the shortlisted layout against the site.
