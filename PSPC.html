11. Calculation basis & limitations

Fault-current model

The model calculates symmetrical 3-phase bolted fault current as I = VLL / (√3 × |Zsource + Ztransformer + Zfeeder|). Source impedance is derived from short-circuit MVA and X/R and referred to the LV bus. Transformer Z% and R% are both calculated on the transformer base; X is then obtained from √(Z²−R²). Feeder positive-sequence R/X is added for the entered fault distance. Maximum case uses c=1.05, transformer Z −7.5% and cold conductor R; minimum case uses c=0.95, transformer Z +7.5% and hot conductor R (×1.2). Sensitivity uses the phase-to-phase minimum (0.866 × three-phase). Breaker duty is multiplied by an X/R factor relative to the standard test power factor; CT saturation uses maximum fault × (1+X/R). This is an engineering approximation, not a full IEC 60909 study.

Inverse-time relay model

IEC mode: t = TMS × k / (M^α − 1). IEEE mode: t = TD × [A/(M^p − 1) + B], where M = I/Is. The curve constants are generic standard-family values. Confirm exact manufacturer curve, minimum operating time, reset behavior, tolerances and setting increments before issuing settings.

High-set selectivity model

For an enabled upstream high-set, the study checks it against the maximum strong-source fault in the next downstream relay's zone. If the upstream high-set is below that downstream through-fault level, the model flags a selectivity concern. For R3, an enabled high-set is checked against the minimum weak-source fault in R3's own zone.

What this tool does not calculate

It does not calculate motor fault contribution, transformer damage (ANSI C57.109) curves, earth-fault/zero-sequence, negative-sequence, arc-flash incident energy, CT saturation/transient performance, directional elements, communication-assisted schemes, motor starting/inrush, transformer energization/inrush, fuse curves, breaker mechanical scatter as a time curve, or formal IEC/IEEE compliance certification.
